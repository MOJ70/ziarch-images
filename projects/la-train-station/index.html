LA Train Station

Building as node: an E Line station under one folded roof

Year
2026
Category
Design Studio
Location
Santa Monica, CA, USA
Course
ARC 605 Architectural Design Studio II · Syracuse University School of Architecture
Supervision
Prof. Greg Corso
Team
Seyed Mojtaba Mohammadi
Term
Spring 2026

The first project of the studio treats a small building as a node in Los Angeles' network of flows. Ahead of the 2028 Olympics, which the city uses to accelerate its Twenty-eight by '28 transport projects, a new light-rail station and transit hub replaces the 17th Street/SMC stop on the E Line in Santa Monica, where the line meets the end of the Expo bike path.

Design

The project began with a tectonic case study of Go Hasegawa's House in a Forest, rebuilt as a model and an exploded drawing to understand how one roof can define a whole space. Abstract projective models then tested folding, spanning and enclosure as operations, translating the case study into a language for the station.

Details

A single folded roof of triangulated, translucent panels gathers the station under one shaded canopy and gives it an identity along the line. Beneath it, freestanding volumes for ticketing, security and administration, a café, a climate-controlled waiting room and restrooms sit along one open circulation spine; the roof is built in layers, from cladding and outer space frame to primary structure, sub-structures and ground.

Building as node

The first project of the studio treats a small building as a node in Los Angeles' network of flows. Ahead of the 2028 Olympics, which the city uses to accelerate its Twenty-eight by '28 transport projects, a new light-rail station and transit hub replaces the 17th Street/SMC stop on the E Line in Santa Monica, where the line meets the end of the Expo bike path.

A single folded roof of triangulated, translucent panels gathers the station under one shaded canopy and gives it an identity along the line. Beneath it, freestanding volumes for ticketing, security and administration, a café, a climate-controlled waiting room and restrooms are arranged along an open circulation spine.

1A · Case study

The project began with a tectonic case study of Go Hasegawa's House in a Forest: its steep roof and thin timber frame were rebuilt as a model and an exploded drawing, to understand how one roof can define a whole space.

1B · Projective models

Abstract models tested folding, spanning and enclosure as operations, translating the case study's tectonics into a language for the station.

Program

Freestanding volumes sit under the roof along one primary circulation spine: ticketing hall, security and administration office, retail concession and café, fare-control zone, open-air and climate-controlled waiting areas, and restrooms.

Roof in layers

The folded roof spans the platform and the freestanding volumes beneath it, with the outer space frame above the primary structure.
